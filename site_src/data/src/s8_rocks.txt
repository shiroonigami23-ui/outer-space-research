# Meteorite types|🌠
Chondrite|Stony meteorite|Undifferentiated asteroids|The most common meteorites (~86%), full of chondrules: frozen droplets from the solar nebula 4.56 billion years ago.|Chondrite
Carbonaceous chondrite|Stony meteorite|Primitive carbon-rich asteroids|Rich in water and organic molecules, including amino acids; Murchison and Allende are famous examples.|Carbonaceous_chondrite
Achondrite|Stony meteorite|Melted asteroids, Moon, Mars|Igneous meteorites from bodies that melted.|Achondrite
HED meteorites|Achondrite|Asteroid Vesta|Howardites, eucrites and diogenites, pieces of Vesta's crust.|HED_meteorite
Iron meteorite|Iron meteorite|Cores of shattered asteroids|Iron-nickel alloys showing Widmanstätten patterns that form only with million-year slow cooling.|Iron_meteorite
Pallasite|Stony-iron meteorite|Core–mantle boundary of asteroids|Gem-quality olivine crystals set in iron-nickel metal.|Pallasite
Mesosiderite|Stony-iron meteorite|Asteroid collisions|Breccias of metal and basaltic rock.|Mesosiderite
Martian meteorite|Achondrite|Mars|About 400 known, blasted off Mars by impacts; ALH84001 sparked the 1996 'fossil' debate.|Martian_meteorite
Lunar meteorite|Achondrite|Moon|Rocks thrown from the Moon by impacts and found mostly in deserts and Antarctica.|Lunar_meteorite
Hoba meteorite|Iron meteorite (ataxite)|Asteroid core|The largest known intact meteorite, ~60 tonnes, still in Namibia where it fell.|Hoba_meteorite
Murchison meteorite|Carbonaceous chondrite|Primitive asteroid|Fell in Australia in 1969; contains over 90 amino acids and presolar grains older than the Sun.|Murchison_meteorite
Allende meteorite|Carbonaceous chondrite|Primitive asteroid|Contains calcium-aluminium inclusions, the oldest solids in the solar system (~4.567 billion years).|Allende_meteorite
Chelyabinsk meteor|Chondrite (LL5)|Near-Earth asteroid|A ~20 m asteroid that exploded over Russia in 2013, injuring ~1,500 people with its shock wave.|Chelyabinsk_meteor
Tunguska event|Airburst|Asteroid or comet|A 1908 airburst that flattened ~2,000 km² of Siberian forest.|Tunguska_event
Lonar crater|Impact crater|Basaltic impact|A ~1.8 km crater lake in Maharashtra, India, one of the few impact craters in basalt, about 570,000 years old.|Lonar_Lake
# Space rocks & minerals|🪨
Lunar basalt|Igneous rock|Moon maria|Dark volcanic rock filling the Moon's 'seas'; Apollo returned 382 kg of Moon rock.|Lunar_mare
Anorthosite|Igneous rock|Moon highlands|Bright rock of the lunar highlands, crust from a primordial magma ocean.|Anorthosite
KREEP|Lunar geochemical component|Moon|Rich in potassium, rare-earth elements and phosphorus; concentrated on the near side.|KREEP
Regolith|Soil|Moon, Mars, asteroids|Loose layer of dust and broken rock created by micrometeorite bombardment; lunar dust is sharp and clingy.|Regolith
Jarosite|Mineral|Mars|A sulfate mineral that forms only in acidic water, found by Opportunity.|Jarosite
Hematite 'blueberries'|Mineral|Mars|Small spherical concretions found by Opportunity, evidence of ancient groundwater.|Martian_spherules
Vivianite and greigite|Minerals|Mars (Jezero Crater)|Iron minerals in Sapphire Canyon's 'leopard spots' that on Earth are often made by microbes.|Cheyava_Falls
Moissanite|Mineral|Meteorites|Silicon carbide, first found in a meteorite crater; presolar grains of it came from dying stars.|Moissanite
Tranquillityite|Mineral|Moon|One of three minerals first discovered in Apollo 11 samples.|Tranquillityite
Ice VII and exotic ices|Ice|Ocean worlds, icy moons|High-pressure ices that may form under deep alien oceans.|Ice_VII
Tholins|Organic compounds|Titan, Pluto, comets|Reddish organic gunk made by UV light on methane and nitrogen.|Tholin
Diamond planets and space diamonds|Carbon|Meteorites, white dwarfs|Nanodiamonds in meteorites; crystallised white dwarfs may be like giant diamonds.|Diamond
