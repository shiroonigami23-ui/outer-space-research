# Interstellar Objects and Asteroids

Status date: November 15, 2026.

## The three interstellar objects

| Object | Found | Eccentricity e | v∞ (km/s) | Nature |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| 1I/ʻOumuamua | Oct 2017 | ≈1.20 | ≈26 | No visible coma; elongated; non-gravitational acceleration |
| 2I/Borisov | Aug 2019 | ≈3.36 | ≈32 | Clearly a comet; CO-rich |
| **3I/ATLAS** (C/2025 N1) | **1 Jul 2025** | **≈6.1** | **≈58** | Active comet; reddish dust; became active unusually far out |

## 3I/ATLAS in detail

- **Discovery:** ATLAS survey telescope at Río Hurtado, Chile, against the dense star fields of the galactic plane. Pre-discovery images go back to May–June 2025 (ZTF, TESS, ATLAS), and Rubin caught it during science validation.
- **Orbit (Seligman et al. 2025):** e ≈ 6.1, perihelion q ≈ 1.36 AU, inclination ≈ 175° (retrograde), v∞ ≈ 58 km/s.
- **Perihelion:** 29 Oct 2025 at ~1.35 AU, behind the Sun as seen from Earth. Space-based coronagraph data showed **unusually steep brightening** near perihelion. It became observable from the ground again from mid/late November 2025.
- **Activity:** TESS data show it was active at ≳6 AU, far beyond typical water-ice sublimation distances. CO₂-driven activity is the leading explanation.
- **Age and origin:** its kinematics fit the Milky Way's thin or thick disk. A thick-disk origin would make it possibly **>7 billion years old**, older than the Solar System.
- **Size:** early H ≈ 12 suggested a nucleus up to ~10 km, but a bright coma inflates that. Later work argues for a much smaller nucleus (<~1 km).

## Open Questions

- How common are interstellar objects? LSST should give the first real population statistics.
- Why did 3I/ATLAS brighten so steeply at perihelion: a volatile mix, fragmentation, or something else?
- Can a future "interceptor" mission (ESA Comet Interceptor, launch ~2029) reach one?
