## The near future (billions of years)

- **~1 billion years:** the brightening Sun boils away Earth's oceans.
- **~4–5 billion years:** the Milky Way and Andromeda may merge into one elliptical galaxy ('Milkomeda'). A 2025 study put the chance of a merger within 10 billion years at about 50%.
- **~5 billion years:** the Sun becomes a red giant, then a white dwarf.

## The far future

| Era | When | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Stelliferous era | Now – ~100 trillion years | Stars keep forming until gas runs out; red dwarfs shine for trillions of years. |
| Degenerate era | ~10¹⁴ – 10⁴⁰ years | Only white dwarfs, neutron stars, brown dwarfs and black holes remain; protons may decay. |
| Black hole era | ~10⁴⁰ – 10¹⁰⁰ years | Black holes dominate, slowly evaporating via Hawking radiation. |
| Dark era | >10¹⁰⁰ years | A cold, dilute sea of photons and particles. |

## Possible endings

- **Heat death (Big Freeze):** with a constant dark energy, expansion continues for ever and the universe reaches maximum entropy. This is the leading expectation.
- **Big Rip:** if dark energy grows stronger ('phantom' energy), it would eventually tear apart galaxies, stars and atoms.
- **Big Crunch:** if dark energy weakens and reverses, expansion could stop and collapse. DESI's hint of weakening dark energy has revived interest in this.
- **Vacuum decay:** if the Higgs field sits in a 'false vacuum', a bubble of lower energy could spread at light speed. It is possible in theory and extremely unlikely on any relevant timescale.
