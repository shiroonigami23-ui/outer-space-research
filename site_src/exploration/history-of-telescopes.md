## Before telescopes

Naked-eye astronomers built precise instruments: Hipparchus's star catalogue (~150 BCE), Ulugh Beg's Samarkand observatory (1420s), Tycho Brahe's measurements (1570s–90s) that Kepler used to discover elliptical orbits, and Jai Singh II's **Jantar Mantar** observatories in India (1720s–30s).

## The first telescopes

- **1608:** Dutch spectacle-maker **Hans Lippershey** applied for a patent on a 'device for seeing far'.
- **1609–10:** **Galileo Galilei** built his own (~20×) and found Jupiter's four big moons, the phases of Venus, mountains on the Moon and countless stars in the Milky Way, evidence that helped overturn the Earth-centred universe.
- **1611:** **Kepler** designed a better refractor with two convex lenses.
- **1668:** **Isaac Newton** built the first practical reflecting telescope, using a mirror to avoid colour fringes.

## Bigger and bigger

| Year | Telescope | Discovery |
|---|---|---|
| 1781 | Herschel's reflector | Uranus, the first planet found by telescope |
| 1845 | Leviathan of Parsonstown | Spiral 'nebulae' (galaxies) |
| 1917 | Hooker 2.5 m | Hubble shows galaxies are separate and the universe expands |
| 1948 | Hale 5 m | Quasars, stellar populations |
| 1993 | Keck 10 m | Segmented mirrors, adaptive optics |
| 2029 (planned) | ELT 39 m | Earth-like exoplanet atmospheres |

## Beyond visible light

- **Radio:** Karl Jansky (1932) heard the Milky Way; radio telescopes found pulsars, quasars and the CMB. India's **GMRT** near Pune is one of the largest low-frequency arrays.
- **Space telescopes:** Hubble (1990), Chandra (X-ray), Spitzer and **JWST** (infrared, 2021), Gaia, Euclid, and **Roman** (launched August 2026).
- **New messengers:** neutrinos (IceCube), gravitational waves (LIGO, with LIGO-India under construction).
