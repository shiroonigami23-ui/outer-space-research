Dark Matter and Dark Energy: The 95% We Can't See

Ordinary matter is under 5% of the universe. The rest is dark matter (~27%) and dark energy (~68%), and in 2025 DESI hinted that dark energy may be changing.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 1 min read🔗 0 sources🎓 Intermediate

Dark matter#

  • Discovery: Fritz Zwicky (1933) found galaxies in the Coma cluster moving too fast; Vera Rubin and Kent Ford (1970s) showed spiral galaxies rotate too fast at their edges.
  • Evidence today: gravitational lensing, the Bullet Cluster (mass separated from hot gas), CMB fluctuation patterns, and the growth of cosmic structure.
  • Candidates: WIMPs (not yet found in direct-detection experiments such as LZ), axions, sterile neutrinos and primordial black holes. Modified-gravity theories such as MOND explain some galaxy data but struggle with clusters and the CMB.

Dark energy#

In 1998 two teams measuring Type Ia supernovae found the expansion of the universe is accelerating (Nobel Prize 2011). The simplest explanation is Einstein’s cosmological constant, the energy of empty space.

Is dark energy evolving?

The DESI collaboration’s 2025 DR2 baryon-acoustic-oscillation results, combined with supernovae and the CMB, prefer a dark energy that weakens over time at 2.8–4.2σ, depending on which supernova sample is used. That is suggestive but below the 5σ discovery threshold. Euclid and Rubin will test it. See DESI 2025 analysis.

Why it matters#

Dark energy decides the fate of the universe; dark matter decides how galaxies formed. Together they are the biggest unsolved problem in physics. See The fate of the universe.
