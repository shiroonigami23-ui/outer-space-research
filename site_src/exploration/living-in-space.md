## Animals went first

Fruit flies (1947), monkeys (1949), dogs (**Laika**, 1957, the first to orbit; **Belka and Strelka**, 1960, the first to return), chimpanzee **Ham** (1961), **Félicette** the cat (1963), tortoises around the Moon (Zond 5, 1968), spiders, fish, jellyfish, mice and tardigrades, which survived open space. See the [People & Animals](/wiki/people/index.html) catalog.

## What space does to the body

| Effect | Cause | Countermeasure |
|---|---|---|
| Bone loss (~1–1.5% per month) | No load on bones | ~2 hours of exercise daily |
| Muscle loss | Microgravity | Resistance machines |
| Fluid shift, 'puffy face', vision changes (SANS) | Fluids move to the head | Under study |
| Radiation | Cosmic rays, solar storms | Shielding, storm shelters |
| Space motion sickness | Confused inner ear | Adapts in days |
| Taller by up to ~5 cm | Spine decompresses | Returns on landing |

NASA's **Twins Study** (Scott and Mark Kelly, 2015–16) showed most changes reverse after a year in space.

## Records

- Longest single flight: **Valeri Polyakov**, 437 days on Mir (1994–95).
- Most cumulative time: **Oleg Kononenko**, over 1,000 days (2024).
- Farthest from Earth: the **Artemis II** crew, 406,771 km (April 2026).

## India in human spaceflight

**Rakesh Sharma** flew in 1984. **Shubhanshu Shukla** became the first Indian aboard the ISS on Axiom-4 (2025). ISRO's **Gaganyaan** programme plans the uncrewed G1 flight, then India's first crewed launch in 2027.
