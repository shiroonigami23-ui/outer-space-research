# Seligman et al. (2025): Discovery and preliminary characterization of a third interstellar object, 3I/ATLAS

- **Citation:** Seligman D.Z. + 46 co-authors, arXiv:2507.02757 (3 Jul 2025, v3 24 Jul 2025); published in *ApJL*
- **Access:** 🔓 arXiv
- **Analysis basis:** arXiv abstract and HTML plus follow-up literature digests.

## Question
Is the new object C/2025 N1 really interstellar, and what is it like?

## Method
- Astrometry from ATLAS discovery and pre-discovery (ZTF, ATLAS) data; deep stacks with CFHT; colour photometry.

## Key Results
- **e ≈ 6.1, q ≈ 1.36 AU, i ≈ 175°, v∞ ≈ 58 km/s**, unambiguously interstellar.
- Faint activity (coma) already visible. Red visible/NIR slope, comparable to ʻOumuamua.
- H_V ≈ 12 suggested up to ~10 km, but this is inflated by the coma.
- Observability gap near perihelion (Sep–Nov 2025), so the authors called for rapid, coordinated characterisation, including from Mars-based spacecraft.

## What happened next (from later papers)
- Active at ≳6 AU in TESS data; reddening coma; possible thick-disk origin (could be >7 Gyr old).
- Steep brightening at perihelion (29 Oct 2025) seen by space coronagraphs.

## Strengths
- Fast, community-wide characterisation with clear orbital solutions.

## Limitations
- Size estimates are model-dependent while a coma is present.
- Origin claims are statistical (kinematics), not tied to any specific star.

## My Take
3I/ATLAS turns interstellar objects from curiosities into a sample of three with very different behaviour. LSST should turn that into a population study within a few years.
