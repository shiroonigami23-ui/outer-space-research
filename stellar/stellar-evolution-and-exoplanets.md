# Stellar Evolution and Exoplanets

Status date: November 15, 2026.

## Concepts

- **Hydrostatic balance**
  - A star is a fight between gravity pulling in and gas plus radiation pressure pushing out. Fusion in the core supplies the pressure.
- **Mass–luminosity relation**
  - On the main sequence, L ∝ M^~3.5. Doubling a star's mass makes it ~11× brighter.
- **Lifetime scaling**
  - Fuel ∝ M and burn rate ∝ L, so lifetime t ≈ 10 Gyr × (M/M☉)^-2.5.
  - Sun ≈ 10 Gyr, a 10 M☉ star ≈ 30 Myr, a 0.5 M☉ red dwarf ≈ 57 Gyr (longer than the universe has existed).
- **End states by initial mass (working)**
  - < ~8 M☉: red giant → planetary nebula → white dwarf (the Sun's fate).
  - ~8–20 M☉: core-collapse supernova → neutron star.
  - > ~20 M☉: supernova or direct collapse → black hole.
- **Hertzsprung–Russell diagram**
  - Plots luminosity against surface temperature. Main-sequence stars lie on a diagonal band; giants sit above it and white dwarfs below it (see `png/hr_diagram_generated.png`).

## Exoplanet Core

- **Detection methods**
  - Transit: the dip in starlight as a planet crosses its star. More than two-thirds of confirmed planets.
  - Radial velocity: the star wobbles towards and away from us.
  - Microlensing, direct imaging and timing make up the rest.
- **Count**
  - The NASA Exoplanet Archive passed **6,000 confirmed planets** in September 2025 (6,007 at the time of the announcement), with more than 8,000 candidates awaiting confirmation. It had reached 5,000 in March 2022.
- **Why red dwarfs dominate rocky-planet studies**
  - Small star + small planet = deep transit; close-in habitable zones = frequent transits. Examples: TRAPPIST-1e and Proxima b (see [`../planetary/png/habitable_zones_generated.png`](../planetary/png/habitable_zones_generated.png)).
  - Downside: flares and strong winds may strip the atmospheres of planets that close to the star.

## Open Questions

- Can rocky planets around active M dwarfs keep their atmospheres? JWST thermal-emission results on TRAPPIST-1 b and c so far point to thin or absent atmospheres.
- What sets the "radius valley" between super-Earths and sub-Neptunes: photoevaporation or core-powered mass loss?
- How common are true Earth analogues (Earth-size, 1 AU orbit, Sun-like star)? Current surveys are biased against finding them.
