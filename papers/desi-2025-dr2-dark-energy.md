# DESI Collaboration (2025): DR2 Results II — BAO measurements and cosmological constraints

- **Citation:** DESI Collaboration, *DESI DR2 Results II: Measurements of Baryon Acoustic Oscillations and Cosmological Constraints* (arXiv:2503.14738, released 19 March 2025)
- **Access:** 🔓 arXiv
- **Analysis basis:** abstract, collaboration and university releases (OSU, UT Dallas, IFAE, Ohio U.) and coverage. Full text not read here.

## Question
Does the expansion history traced by baryon acoustic oscillations still fit a cosmological constant, or does dark energy change over time?

## Method
- Three years of spectroscopic data: ~15 million galaxies and quasars, including Lyman-α forest BAO at high z. The dataset is more than twice the size of DR1.
- **Blinded analysis**, so the results stay hidden from the team until the pipeline is frozen.
- Fits ΛCDM and the w0–wa (CPL) dark-energy model, alone and combined with CMB (Planck/ACT) and supernovae (Pantheon+, Union3, DESY5).

## Key Results
- **DESI alone is consistent with ΛCDM.**
- **DESI + CMB + supernovae prefer evolving dark energy at 2.8–4.2σ**, depending on the supernova sample. This is stronger than DR1.
- The preferred region has w0 > −1 and wa < 0. Dark energy would have been stronger in the recent past and is weakening today. There is a possible "phantom crossing" (w < −1) at earlier times; see [`../cosmology/png/dark_energy_evolution_generated.png`](../cosmology/png/dark_energy_evolution_generated.png).
- DR1 public data released alongside.

## Strengths
- Largest 3D map yet; blinding; multiple independent tracers.
- Several different external datasets point the same way.

## Limitations and Critiques
- **Not 5σ.** Significance depends heavily on *which supernova compilation* is used, which suggests supernova calibration systematics could drive part of the signal.
- The w0–wa form is a convenient fit, not a physical model. Different parameterisations can shift the conclusion.
- Internal team members themselves call it "too early to claim categorically".

## My Take
This is the most important open question in cosmology right now. The fact that DESI *alone* agrees with Λ, while the tension appears only in combinations, makes cross-dataset systematics the first suspect. Euclid DR1 (late 2026) and the DESI final data are the independent checks.

## Follow-ups
- Repeat with recalibrated supernova samples, and with Euclid weak lensing.
- Test physically motivated models (quintessence, interacting dark energy) instead of CPL.
