# Parker Solar Probe and Space Weather

Status date: November 15, 2026.

## Concepts

- **Corona problem:** the Sun's outer atmosphere is >1 million °C while the surface is ~5,500 °C. Leading explanations are wave heating and nanoflares or magnetic reconnection.
- **Solar wind:** a continuous outflow of plasma at ~300–800 km/s. "Switchbacks" (sudden magnetic-field reversals) were a Parker discovery.
- **Space weather:** CMEs and solar energetic particles can disrupt satellites, GPS and power grids, and are a radiation risk to astronauts (relevant to Artemis).

## Parker Solar Probe records

| Date | Distance from surface | Speed |
|---|---|---|
| Sep 2023 | 7.26 million km | 635,266 km/h |
| **24 Dec 2024** | **6.1 million km** | **≈692,000 km/h** (fastest human-made object) |
| 22 Mar 2025, 19 Jun 2025 | ≈6.1 million km (repeat) | ≈692,000 km/h |
| 11 Mar 2026 (27th perihelion) | ≈6.2 million km (repeat) | ≈687,000 km/h |

- The heat shield faced ~980 °C while the instruments stayed near room temperature (~29 °C).
- Seven Venus gravity assists, the last in Nov 2024, shrank the orbit to this final perihelion.
- The mission continues observing into the declining phase of the solar cycle. Its next steps beyond late 2026 are under NASA review.

## Science highlights (working list)

- Measurements through **CME-driven shocks** are revealing how hazardous solar energetic particles are accelerated.
- Passing through a CME confirmed a 20-year-old idea about **CMEs sweeping interplanetary dust**.

## Open Questions

- Which mechanism dominates coronal heating?
- Can in-situ data improve CME arrival and severity forecasts enough to protect crewed lunar missions?
