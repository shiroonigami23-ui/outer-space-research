## A ladder of distances

| Step | Distance | Light-travel time |
|---|---|---|
| Earth to Moon | 384,400 km | 1.3 seconds |
| Earth to Sun (1 AU) | 150 million km | 8.3 minutes |
| Sun to Neptune | 30 AU | ~4 hours |
| Voyager 1 (Nov 2026) | ~173 AU | **1 light-day**, the first human object this far |
| Oort Cloud outer edge | ~100,000 AU | ~1.5 years |
| Nearest star (Proxima Centauri) | 4.24 light-years | 4.24 years |
| Across the Milky Way | ~100,000 light-years | — |
| Andromeda Galaxy | 2.5 million light-years | — |
| Laniakea Supercluster | ~520 million light-years across | — |
| Observable universe | **~93 billion light-years** across | — |

The observable universe is larger than 13.8 billion light-years in radius because space has expanded while the light was travelling: the most distant regions we see are now ~46 billion light-years away.

## How we measure: the cosmic distance ladder

1. **Radar and spacecraft** within the solar system.
2. **Parallax**: the shift of nearby stars as Earth orbits; Gaia measured ~2 billion stars.
3. **Standard candles**: Cepheid variables, the tip of the red giant branch, and Type Ia supernovae.
4. **Redshift**: Hubble's law converts recession speed into distance.

Disagreement between rungs of this ladder is at the heart of the **Hubble tension**.

## How empty is space?

- If the Sun were a grapefruit in London, Proxima Centauri would be another grapefruit in Moscow.
- The interstellar medium averages about **1 atom per cubic centimetre**; intergalactic space about **1 atom per cubic metre**, emptier than any lab vacuum.
- **Cosmic voids** such as the Boötes Void span ~330 million light-years with very few galaxies. See [Cosmic void](/wiki/objects/cosmic-void.html).
- Even 'empty' space carries the CMB, neutrinos, magnetic fields, dark matter, dark energy and quantum vacuum fluctuations.

## Is the universe infinite?

We don't know. Measurements show space is flat to within ~0.4%, which is consistent with an infinite universe or a finite one far larger than we can see.
