# Gravitational Waves and Black Holes

Status date: November 15, 2026.

## Concepts

- **Gravitational waves:** ripples in spacetime from accelerating masses. LIGO's 4 km arms change length by less than a proton's width as a wave passes.
- **Three phases of a merger signal:**
  1. **Inspiral:** a "chirp" as frequency and amplitude rise.
  2. **Merger.**
  3. **Ringdown:** the new black hole settles, emitting quasi-normal modes ("tones").
- **Kerr metric:** a spinning black hole is described completely by mass and spin. Its ringdown frequencies are fixed by those two numbers, which makes the ringdown a direct test.
- **Hawking area theorem (1971):** in classical general relativity, total event-horizon area never decreases. Bekenstein and Hawking tied that area to **entropy**, so this is the black-hole second law of thermodynamics.

## GW250114 (LVK, *Phys. Rev. Lett.* 135, 111403, Sept 2025)

- Signal-to-noise ratio ≈ **80** in the two LIGO detectors.
- Near-equal masses: **33.6 and 32.2 M☉**, small spins (χ ≤ 0.26), negligible eccentricity.
- **Area law:**
  - Pre-merger total ≈ 240,000 km². My check from the published masses gives ≈237,000 km² for non-spinning horizons (see the figure).
  - Remnant ≈ 400,000 km².
  - Result consistent with the area law at ~99.999% credibility. The 2021 test with GW150914 reached ~95%.
- **Kerr test:** the dominant (ℓ=|m|=2) mode **and its first overtone** were identified, with frequencies matching Kerr predictions to within ±30%.

## Why it matters

- It is the cleanest empirical check yet of black-hole thermodynamics, where gravity meets quantum physics.
- Louder signals mean better tests of general relativity. Next-generation detectors (Cosmic Explorer, Einstein Telescope, LISA) will push this to "spectroscopy" of many modes.

## Open Questions

- How do black holes in the "pair-instability gap" (~60–130 M☉) form? Hierarchical mergers are one possibility.
- Is there any deviation from Kerr at higher overtone precision?
