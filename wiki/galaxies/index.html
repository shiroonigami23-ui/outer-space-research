🌌 Galaxies & Nebulae

Galaxies from the Milky Way to the most distant ever seen, plus famous nebulae, star clusters and superclusters.

43 entries in 2 groups.

Galaxies

Milky Way

Our home galaxy, with 100–400 billion stars and a 4-million-solar-mass black hole at its centre.

Galaxies

Andromeda Galaxy (M31)

The nearest large galaxy, visible to the naked eye; may merge with the Milky Way in ~4–5 billion years.

Galaxies

Triangulum Galaxy (M33)

Third-largest Local Group galaxy.

Galaxies

Large Magellanic Cloud

Satellite galaxy hosting the Tarantula Nebula and SN 1987A.

Galaxies

Small Magellanic Cloud

Companion of the LMC, being torn apart.

Galaxies

Sagittarius Dwarf

Being shredded by the Milky Way; its passages may have triggered spiral arms.

Galaxies

Whirlpool Galaxy (M51)

Classic grand-design spiral tugging on a companion.

Galaxies

Sombrero Galaxy (M104)

A bright bulge and dust lane like a hat; JWST showed its clumpy ring.

Galaxies

M87

Home of the first black hole ever imaged (2019) and a 5,000-light-year jet.

Galaxies

Centaurus A

The nearest active galaxy, with radio lobes a million light-years long.

Galaxies

Cartwheel Galaxy

Shaped by a head-on collision with a smaller galaxy.

Galaxies

Antennae Galaxies

Two galaxies colliding, igniting bursts of star formation.

Galaxies

Pinwheel Galaxy (M101)

Hosted supernova SN 2023ixf, the closest in a decade.

Galaxies

Cigar Galaxy (M82)

Forming stars 10× faster than the Milky Way, blowing out superwinds.

Galaxies

Hoag's Object

An almost perfect ring galaxy of unknown origin.

Galaxies

IC 1101

One of the largest galaxies known.

Galaxies

GN-z11

Seen 400 million years after the Big Bang, with an active black hole.

Galaxies

JADES-GS-z14-0

Confirmed at z = 14.3 (2024), about 290 million years after the Big Bang.

Galaxies

MoM-z14

The most distant confirmed galaxy (z = 14.44), ~280 million years after the Big Bang (2025–26).

Galaxies

Dragonfly 44

A galaxy thought to be mostly dark matter.

Galaxies

NGC 1052-DF2

Appeared to have almost no dark matter.

Galaxies

Phoenix Cluster

A cluster with a central galaxy forming stars at a record rate.

Galaxies

Bullet Cluster

Colliding clusters whose separated mass and gas offer key evidence for dark matter.

Galaxies

El Gordo

The largest known distant galaxy cluster.

Galaxies

Virgo Cluster

Over 1,300 galaxies; centre of our Local Supercluster.

Galaxies

Laniakea Supercluster

Our home supercluster (defined 2014), containing ~100,000 galaxies.

Galaxies

Hercules–Corona Borealis Great Wall

Possibly the largest structure known in the universe.

Nebulae & clusters

Orion Nebula (M42)

The nearest massive star-forming region, visible to the naked eye.

Nebulae & clusters

Pillars of Creation (Eagle Nebula)

Iconic columns of gas imaged by Hubble (1995) and JWST (2022).

Nebulae & clusters

Crab Nebula (M1)

Remains of the supernova recorded by Chinese astronomers in 1054.

Nebulae & clusters

Carina Nebula

Home to Eta Carinae; its 'Cosmic Cliffs' were among JWST's first images.

Nebulae & clusters

Ring Nebula (M57)

A dying Sun-like star's shell.

Nebulae & clusters

Helix Nebula

The 'Eye of God', one of the nearest planetary nebulae.

Nebulae & clusters

Cat's Eye Nebula

One of the most complex planetary nebulae.

Nebulae & clusters

Horsehead Nebula

A dark dust cloud shaped like a horse's head.

Nebulae & clusters

Tarantula Nebula

The most active starburst region in the Local Group.

Nebulae & clusters

Lagoon Nebula (M8)

Bright naked-eye star nursery.

Nebulae & clusters

Veil Nebula

Delicate filaments from a supernova ~10,000 years ago.

Nebulae & clusters

Cassiopeia A

The youngest known Milky Way supernova remnant (~340 years).

Nebulae & clusters

Pleiades (M45)

The Seven Sisters, a young cluster known to cultures worldwide (Krittika in India).

Nebulae & clusters

Omega Centauri

The largest globular cluster, possibly the core of a swallowed galaxy, hosting an intermediate-mass black hole.

Nebulae & clusters

47 Tucanae

A dense ball of ~a million old stars.

Hyades
