level: Advanced

:::warning Theoretical only
There is **no observational evidence** for white holes or traversable wormholes. Everything on this page is theory.
:::

## White holes

A **white hole** is the time-reverse of a black hole: nothing can enter its horizon, but matter and light can only come out.

- It appears in the full ('maximally extended') Schwarzschild solution of general relativity, found by Martin Kruskal and George Szekeres in 1960.
- **Problems:** it would be extremely unstable. Any matter falling toward it would pile up and likely collapse it into a black hole. It also seems to violate the second law of thermodynamics.
- **Speculative ideas:** loop quantum gravity models suggest black holes might 'bounce' into white holes at the end of their lives; some have proposed the Big Bang itself was white-hole-like. Some have even suggested that unusual, short gamma-ray bursts could be white holes; there is no support for this.

## Wormholes

A **wormhole** (Einstein–Rosen bridge, 1935) connects two distant regions of spacetime through a 'throat'.

- The original Einstein–Rosen bridge pinches off too fast for anything, even light, to pass through.
- Kip Thorne and Michael Morris (1988) showed that a **traversable wormhole** would need **exotic matter with negative energy** to hold it open. Tiny negative energies exist (the Casimir effect), but nowhere near enough.
- Wormholes could in principle act as time machines, which raises paradoxes; Hawking's **chronology protection conjecture** suggests physics forbids this.
- **ER = EPR:** Juan Maldacena and Leonard Susskind (2013) proposed that quantum entanglement and wormholes may be two sides of the same thing. A 2022 'wormhole on a quantum chip' experiment simulated a toy model; it did not create a real wormhole.

## In fiction

*Interstellar* (2014) used Kip Thorne's equations to render its wormhole and black hole Gargantua, producing a scientifically grounded image of a black hole years before the Event Horizon Telescope.
