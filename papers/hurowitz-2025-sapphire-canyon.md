# Hurowitz et al. (2025): Redox-driven mineral and organic associations in Jezero Crater, Mars

- **Citation:** Hurowitz J.A., et al. *Nature* (10 Sept 2025)
- **Access:** subscription (see the reading list)
- **Analysis basis:** NASA release, press conference coverage and expert Q&A (The Conversation). Full text not read here.

## Question
Do the "leopard spots" in the Cheyava Falls rock (Bright Angel formation) record biological activity?

## Method
- Perseverance's PIXL (X-ray chemistry) and SHERLOC (organics/Raman) mapped textures and minerals in the rock and in the drilled core **Sapphire Canyon** (sample 25, collected July 2024).

## Key Results
- "Leopard spots" are concentric **reaction fronts** where redox-sensitive minerals (iron phosphate and iron sulfide phases) sit with organic carbon in ~3.5-billion-year-old lake mudstone.
- On Earth, similar mineral pairs often form when microbes drive redox reactions. This is classed as a **potential biosignature**, not a detection.
- The finding widens the search to younger sedimentary rocks than expected.

## Strengths
- Multi-instrument, micro-scale context; peer-reviewed in *Nature*; the sample is cached for possible return.

## Limitations and Critiques
- Non-biological, low-temperature reactions could produce similar textures. Ruling that out needs Earth labs (isotopes, nanoscale imaging).
- Mars Sample Return is being restructured or is at risk in budgets, so the decisive tests may be years away.
