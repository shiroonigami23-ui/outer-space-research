🪨 Space Rocks & Minerals

Meteorite types, famous falls and impacts, Moon and Mars rocks and space minerals, and what they reveal about the solar system's birth.

27 entries in 2 groups.

📊 Charts and statistics for this catalog
Meteorite types

Chondrite

The most common meteorites (~86%), full of chondrules: frozen droplets from the solar nebula 4.56 billion years ago.

Meteorite types

Carbonaceous chondrite

Rich in water and organic molecules, including amino acids; Murchison and Allende are famous examples.

Meteorite types

Achondrite

Igneous meteorites from bodies that melted.

Meteorite types

HED meteorites

Howardites, eucrites and diogenites, pieces of Vesta's crust.

Meteorite types

Iron meteorite

Iron-nickel alloys showing Widmanstätten patterns that form only with million-year slow cooling.

Meteorite types

Pallasite

Gem-quality olivine crystals set in iron-nickel metal.

Meteorite types

Mesosiderite

Breccias of metal and basaltic rock.

Meteorite types

Martian meteorite

About 400 known, blasted off Mars by impacts; ALH84001 sparked the 1996 'fossil' debate.

Meteorite types

Lunar meteorite

Rocks thrown from the Moon by impacts and found mostly in deserts and Antarctica.

Meteorite types

Hoba meteorite

The largest known intact meteorite, ~60 tonnes, still in Namibia where it fell.

Meteorite types

Murchison meteorite

Fell in Australia in 1969; contains over 90 amino acids and presolar grains older than the Sun.

Meteorite types

Allende meteorite

Contains calcium-aluminium inclusions, the oldest solids in the solar system (~4.567 billion years).

Meteorite types

Chelyabinsk meteor

A ~20 m asteroid that exploded over Russia in 2013, injuring ~1,500 people with its shock wave.

Meteorite types

Tunguska event

A 1908 airburst that flattened ~2,000 km² of Siberian forest.

Meteorite types

Lonar crater

A ~1.8 km crater lake in Maharashtra, India, one of the few impact craters in basalt, about 570,000 years old.

Space rocks & minerals

Lunar basalt

Dark volcanic rock filling the Moon's 'seas'; Apollo returned 382 kg of Moon rock.

Space rocks & minerals

Anorthosite

Bright rock of the lunar highlands, crust from a primordial magma ocean.

Space rocks & minerals

KREEP

Rich in potassium, rare-earth elements and phosphorus; concentrated on the near side.

Space rocks & minerals

Regolith

Loose layer of dust and broken rock created by micrometeorite bombardment; lunar dust is sharp and clingy.

Space rocks & minerals

Jarosite

A sulfate mineral that forms only in acidic water, found by Opportunity.

Space rocks & minerals

Hematite 'blueberries'

Small spherical concretions found by Opportunity, evidence of ancient groundwater.

Space rocks & minerals

Vivianite and greigite

Iron minerals in Sapphire Canyon's 'leopard spots' that on Earth are often made by microbes.

Space rocks & minerals

Moissanite

Silicon carbide, first found in a meteorite crater; presolar grains of it came from dying stars.

Space rocks & minerals

Tranquillityite

One of three minerals first discovered in Apollo 11 samples.

Space rocks & minerals

Ice VII and exotic ices

High-pressure ices that may form under deep alien oceans.

Space rocks & minerals

Tholins

Reddish organic gunk made by UV light on methane and nitrogen.

Space rocks & minerals

Diamond planets and space diamonds

Nanodiamonds in meteorites; crystallised white dwarfs may be like giant diamonds.
