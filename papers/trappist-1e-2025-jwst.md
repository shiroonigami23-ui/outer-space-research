# Espinoza et al. & Glidden et al. (2025): JWST-TST DREAMS — TRAPPIST-1 e transmission spectroscopy

- **Citations:** Espinoza N., et al. *ApJL* 990, L52 (doi:10.3847/2041-8213/adf42e); Glidden A., et al. *ApJL* 990, L53 (doi:10.3847/2041-8213/adf62e), Sept 2025
- **Access:** 🔓 largely open (AAS)
- **Analysis basis:** abstracts, NASA/STScI release, AAS Nova summary and coverage.

## Question
Does TRAPPIST-1 e, an Earth-size planet in the habitable zone 40 light-years away, keep an atmosphere?

## Method
- Four JWST NIRSpec/PRISM transits (2023). Retrievals across many atmospheric compositions and pressures; modelling of stellar contamination.

## Key Results
- **No primordial H₂/He atmosphere**, likely stripped by the flaring star.
- A **CO₂-dominated** atmosphere (Venus- or Mars-like) is **unlikely**.
- **Trace CH₄ in an N₂-rich atmosphere fits acceptably**, but the data **cannot reject a bare rock**.
- **Starspots** on TRAPPIST-1 imprint features that mimic or mask planetary ones.

## Strengths
- Careful null-hypothesis framing; explicit treatment of the star.

## Limitations
- Only four transits. Different visits disagree (July vs October 2023), so stellar variability matters.

## What's next
- About 15 more transits, including **back-to-back transits with TRAPPIST-1 b** (airless) to subtract the star's contribution. Roughly 20 transits are expected in total.

## My Take
This is the best near-term test of whether rocky planets around M dwarfs can stay habitable. The b/e differencing trick is clever, and the result will shape the target list for the Habitable Worlds Observatory.
