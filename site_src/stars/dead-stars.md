## White dwarfs

The cores of Sun-like stars: about the Sun's mass squeezed into an Earth-sized ball, held up by **electron degeneracy pressure**. Subrahmanyan Chandrasekhar (from Lahore, then British India) calculated in 1930 that none can exceed ~1.4 solar masses, the **Chandrasekhar limit** (Nobel Prize 1983). As they cool, their carbon-oxygen interiors **crystallise**. See [White dwarf](/wiki/objects/white-dwarf.html).

## Neutron stars

Collapsed cores of massive stars, ~20 km across but 1.2–2.3 times the Sun's mass. Protons and electrons are crushed into neutrons. Surface gravity is ~100 billion times Earth's, and mountains are millimetres high.

## Pulsars

Spinning neutron stars sweeping beams across Earth. **Jocelyn Bell Burnell** found the first in 1967 as a research student. Pulsars are such precise clocks that:
- the Hulse–Taylor binary pulsar gave the first indirect evidence of gravitational waves (Nobel 1993);
- pulsar timing arrays (including India's GMRT and InPTA) detected a gravitational-wave background in 2023.

## Magnetars

Neutron stars with magnetic fields ~10¹⁵ gauss, a thousand trillion times Earth's. Starquakes release giant flares; in 2020 a Milky Way magnetar produced a [fast radio burst](/wiki/objects/fast-radio-burst.html).

## Black dwarfs

When a white dwarf has fully cooled it becomes a black dwarf. The universe isn't old enough for any to exist yet.
