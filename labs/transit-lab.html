This is how the transit method finds most known exoplanets. A planet crossing its star blocks a fraction (Rp/R★)² of the light. Change the planet size, the impact parameter and limb darkening, and see why Earth-size planets are easy to find around tiny red dwarfs like TRAPPIST-1 and hard to find around Sun-like stars.

Flux is computed by integrating a limb-darkened stellar disk on a grid, which is accurate to a few ppm. Radius ratios: TRAPPIST-1 e 0.92 R⊕ / 0.119 R☉; K2-18 b 2.61 R⊕ / 0.41 R☉. The noise model is white noise only; real data also have stellar spots and instrument systematics (see the K2-18 b debate in this repo). Part of outer-space-research.
