# Freedman et al. (2025): Status report on the Chicago–Carnegie Hubble Program — H₀ with Hubble and JWST

- **Citation:** Freedman W.L., et al. *The Astrophysical Journal* (2025); arXiv:2408.06153
- **Access:** 🔓 arXiv
- **Analysis basis:** arXiv abstract/intro plus coverage (Physics World, Live Science, Space.com).

## Question
Is the Hubble tension real, or an artefact of Cepheid-based distance calibration?

## Method
- JWST observations of ~10–11 nearby galaxies that hosted Type Ia supernovae.
- Three independent distance indicators: **TRGB** (tip of the red-giant branch), **JAGB** (carbon stars) and Cepheids. They are anchored to geometric distances and then used to calibrate supernovae.

## Key Results
- Best combined value **H₀ ≈ 70.39 km/s/Mpc**, which the authors state is consistent with ΛCDM without new physics.
- Their own Cepheid-based value was **72.04**. The team reads this as a hint of Cepheid systematics, crowding in particular.

## Strengths
- Several independent methods on the same JWST data; TRGB is less sensitive to crowding and dust.

## Limitations and Critiques
- **Small sample.** Riess argues the hosts are drawn from the tail of the distribution.
- 70.4 sits between 67 and 73, so it does not make the tension vanish (Scolnic).
- SH0ES' larger JWST Cepheid samples (~1,000 Cepheids, ~2% distances) find no crowding bias large enough to explain the gap.

## My Take
Both teams use JWST, so the dispute is now about calibrator physics and sample selection. Expanding TRGB/JAGB to the full SH0ES host sample would settle it. Methods independent of the distance ladder (lensing time delays, GW sirens) are the cleanest referees.
