# LIGO–Virgo–KAGRA (2025): GW250114 — Testing Hawking's area law and the Kerr nature of black holes

- **Citation:** LVK Collaboration, *Physical Review Letters* 135(11), 111403 (Sept 2025)
- **Access:** 🔓 CC-BY 4.0 (open)
- **Analysis basis:** published abstract, LIGO science summary and coverage.

## Question
Can one very loud merger directly test that (1) total horizon area never decreases and (2) the remnant is a Kerr black hole?

## Method
- Signal detected on 14 Jan 2025 by both LIGO detectors at a network SNR of ~80.
- Separate analyses of inspiral (initial areas) and post-merger ringdown (final area). Some analyses exclude up to five of the strongest merger cycles to avoid modelling bias.
- The ringdown is fitted with Kerr quasi-normal modes.

## Key Results
- Masses 33.6 (+1.2/−0.8) and 32.2 (+0.8/−1.3) M☉; spins ≤ 0.26; eccentricity ≤ 0.03.
- **Final area greater than total initial area** at high credibility (~99.999%), versus ~95% from GW150914 in 2021. Areas go from ≈240,000 km² before to ≈400,000 km² after.
- **The fundamental ℓ=|m|=2 mode and its first overtone** were identified, with frequencies within ±30% of Kerr predictions.

## Strengths
- Very high SNR; tests designed to be robust to merger-phase modelling.
- Near-twin of GW150914, so it shows how far detector sensitivity has improved (~3× clearer).

## Limitations and Critiques
- ±30% on mode frequencies still leaves room for small deviations from Kerr.
- The area law is a classical result. Quantum effects (Hawking radiation) are far too small to see here.
