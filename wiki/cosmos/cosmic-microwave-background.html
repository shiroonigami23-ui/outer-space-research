The Cosmic Microwave Background: Baby Picture of the Universe

The oldest light in the universe, released 380,000 years after the Big Bang, holds the recipe of the cosmos in its tiny ripples.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 1 min read🔗 0 sources🎓 Intermediate

Discovery#

In 1964–65 Arno Penzias and Robert Wilson at Bell Labs could not get rid of a faint hiss in their horn antenna, even after cleaning out pigeon droppings. It came from every direction: the afterglow of the Big Bang, predicted by Ralph Alpher and Robert Herman in 1948. They won the 1978 Nobel Prize.

What it shows#

  • A temperature of 2.725 K, the most perfect blackbody spectrum in nature (COBE, 1992, Nobel 2006).
  • Ripples of about 1 part in 100,000: the seeds of every galaxy.
  • The pattern of those ripples (‘acoustic peaks’) reveals the universe’s age (13.8 billion years), geometry (flat), and contents (~5% ordinary matter, ~27% dark matter, ~68% dark energy), as measured by Planck (2009–13).

Missions#

Mission Years Achievement
COBE 1989–93 Blackbody spectrum; first ripples
WMAP 2001–10 Precise age and composition
Planck 2009–13 Definitive all-sky map
ACT, SPT Ground Fine-scale detail and lensing
Simons Observatory, LiteBIRD Now / 2030s Search for inflation’s gravitational-wave signature

Anomalies#

A large ‘Cold Spot’ and slight alignments of the largest patterns (‘axis of evil’) are debated. Most cosmologists consider them statistical flukes, but they are watched closely.
