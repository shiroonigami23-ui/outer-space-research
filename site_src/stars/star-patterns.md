## Constellations versus asterisms

- The International Astronomical Union recognises **88 constellations** (fixed in 1922, boundaries drawn by Eugène Delporte in 1930) that together tile the entire sky. Browse them all in the [Constellations](/wiki/constellations/index.html) catalog.
- **Asterisms** are popular patterns that are not official constellations: the **Big Dipper** (Saptarishi), the **Summer Triangle**, Orion's Belt, the Teapot in Sagittarius.

## Stars in a pattern aren't neighbours

Stars in a constellation lie at very different distances. In Orion, Betelgeuse is ~550 light-years away and Rigel ~860. They only line up from our viewpoint.

## The sky slowly changes

- **Precession:** Earth's axis wobbles every ~26,000 years, so the pole star changes: Thuban (Draco) for the pyramid builders, Polaris now, Vega in ~12,000 years. This shifted the zodiac relative to the seasons.
- **Proper motion:** stars move. In ~50,000 years the Big Dipper will look noticeably bent.

## Indian astronomy

- The ecliptic is divided into **27 (or 28) nakshatras**, lunar mansions such as Krittika (the Pleiades) and Rohini (Aldebaran).
- **Aryabhata** (499 CE) proposed that Earth rotates on its axis and explained eclipses by shadows. **Varahamihira** and **Brahmagupta** advanced mathematical astronomy.
- The **Jantar Mantar** observatories of Sawai Jai Singh II (1720s–30s) measured time and positions with giant masonry instruments.

## Navigation and culture

Polynesian navigators used rising and setting points of stars; Indigenous Australians read 'dark constellations' such as the Emu in the Sky in the dust lanes of the Milky Way; sailors everywhere used Polaris and the Southern Cross.
