Space Rocks: Meteors, Meteorites, Asteroids and Comets

The difference between a meteor and a meteorite, the main rock types that fall from space, and what they tell us about the birth of the solar system.

📅 Reviewed 2026-11-15⏱ 1 min read🔗 0 sources🎓 Introductory

Words that get mixed up#

Term Meaning
Meteoroid A small rock or dust grain in space
Meteor The streak of light when a meteoroid burns up in the atmosphere (‘shooting star’)
Meteorite A piece that survives and lands
Asteroid A rocky or metallic body, metres to hundreds of km across, mostly between Mars and Jupiter
Comet An icy body that grows a glowing coma and tails near the Sun
Fireball / bolide An exceptionally bright meteor, sometimes exploding (Chelyabinsk 2013)

Kinds of meteorites#

Browse them in the Space Rocks catalog.

  • Stony (~94%): chondrites with chondrules older than the planets, and carbonaceous chondrites rich in water and organics.
  • Iron (~5%): cores of shattered protoplanets.
  • Stony-iron (~1%): pallasites with olivine gems in metal.
  • Planetary: ~400 from Mars and ~700 from the Moon.

Meteor showers#

Earth crosses streams of comet dust: the Perseids (August, Comet Swift–Tuttle), Geminids (December, asteroid 3200 Phaethon) and Leonids (November, Comet Tempel–Tuttle).

Samples brought home#

Apollo and Luna (Moon), Chang’e 5 and 6 (Moon, including the far side), Stardust (comet dust), Hayabusa and Hayabusa2 (asteroids Itokawa and Ryugu), OSIRIS-REx (Bennu, whose samples contain amino acids and all five nucleobases of DNA and RNA). Tianwen-2 is now sampling asteroid Kamoʻoalewa.

India’s impact crater#

Lonar Lake in Maharashtra is a ~1.8 km crater in basalt, a rare analogue for impacts on the Moon and Mars.
